## Who to talk to

One person in the target segment: 16–30, cares how they dress, is not a fashion
professional. A classmate, a sibling, a friend. In person or on a call, in
whatever language is natural — the notes can be written up in English
afterwards.

**Not** a parent being kind, and not someone who already knows the project well
enough to want it to succeed.

## What is being tested

| | |
|---|---|
| **Assumption** | People can recognise a good outfit but cannot name a direction, so they buy single pieces that never combine. |
| **Falsifier** | If they say they already know their style and only want shopping to be cheaper or faster, the premise of Style Me is wrong. |

## The script — 15 minutes

Ask about **what they did**, not what they would do. Let silences run. Do not
pitch the product until question 7, and write quotes down word for word.

1. Tell me about the last time you bought clothes. What did you buy, and why
   that one?
2. How do you decide whether something suits you?
3. Have you ever bought something and then never worn it? What happened there?
4. When you are not sure about a piece, who or what do you ask?
5. In your own words, how would you describe your style? *(Listen for: do they
   have words for it at all, or do they describe brands and shops?)*
6. Has anything ever helped with this — an app, Pinterest, a salesperson, a
   friend?
7. *Now show `/research` and `/core`.* If a page asked you to describe how you
   dress in one sentence, then named it and dressed it, would you use it? What
   would make it useless to you?
8. Would you rather have something that helps you choose, or just cheaper and
   faster shopping? *(This is the falsifier. Take the answer seriously.)*
9. Is there anything I should have asked?
