# Human Validation Conversation — Week 2

**Required by the assignment:** 1 real human validation conversation.
**Held:** 24 September 2026, in person, about 15 minutes.
**Who:** a French student on the same exchange in Mexico City. Not a fashion
person; buys mostly second-hand. No name recorded, and his age was not asked —
the first thing the script got wrong.
**Language:** the conversation was in French. Quotes are verbatim, with a
translation next to them.

---

## What was being tested

| | |
|---|---|
| **Assumption** | People can recognise a good outfit but cannot name a direction, so they buy single pieces that never combine. |
| **Falsifier** | If they say they already know their style and only want shopping to be cheaper or faster, the premise of Style Me is wrong. |

The product was not shown until question 7, so that the first six answers
describe what he does today rather than a reaction to the demo.

---

## The falsifier triggered

> **« Acheter moins cher et plus vite, 100 %. Franchement, je sais à peu près
> ce que j'aime mettre. Mon vrai problème tous les matins, c'est pas
> l'inspiration, c'est mon budget. »**
>
> *"Cheaper and faster, 100%. Honestly, I roughly know what I like wearing. My
> real problem every morning isn't inspiration, it's my budget."*

That is the falsifier, in the words the packet predicted, and it did not come
out of nowhere. Asked to describe his style — question 5, before seeing
anything — he answered immediately and precisely:

> **« Relax et ample. Un jean large, un t-shirt basique oversize, une bonne
> paire de sneakers. Zéro prise de tête. »**
>
> *"Relaxed and loose. Wide jeans, a basic oversized tee, a good pair of
> sneakers. No fuss."*

He did not grope for words. He had them. The whole premise of the Style Core —
that people recognise a good outfit but cannot name a direction — is wrong for
this person, and he is squarely in the target segment.

## What kept the assumption alive

The same person, three questions earlier:

> **« Grave ! Une chemise un peu trop cintrée que j'avais prise pour un gala.
> Je l'ai mise dix minutes chez moi, je me suis regardé, je me trouvais
> ridicule. Elle pourrit dans mon armoire à Lille depuis. »**
>
> *"Oh yes! A shirt that was a bit too fitted, bought for a ball. I wore it ten
> minutes at home, looked at myself, thought I looked ridiculous. It's been
> rotting in my wardrobe in Lille ever since."*

So: he can name his style, and he still bought a garment he wore once. The
problem is real in what he **did** and wrong in how I **framed** it. He does
not need a vocabulary. He needed something to tell him, before paying, that a
fitted shirt was not going to work on him.

That distinction is the finding of the week, and no source could have produced
it.

## What would make it useless — his words

> **« S'il me propose des tenues avec des fringues que je n'ai pas, ou pire,
> des trucs qui coûtent une blinde. Si je dois lâcher 150 balles pour
> ressembler à l'image, je passe mon tour. »**
>
> *"If it suggests outfits with clothes I don't own, or worse, things that cost
> a fortune. If I have to drop €150 to look like the picture, I'm out."*

The Style Core's budget chips start at €150 — his walk-away number is the
cheapest option the product offers. And his counter-offer was specific:

> **« Si ton site me trouve la même tenue sur Vinted pour 15 euros, là je signe
> tous les jours. »**
>
> *"If your site finds me the same outfit on Vinted for €15, I'm in every day."*

## Where he already goes, and how long he would stay

- **Asking a friend, confirmed:** *« J'envoie un petit snap dans le groupe des
  potes, ou je demande à ma copine. »* — "I send a snap to the group chat, or I
  ask my girlfriend." This is the row that was waiting for a source; it now has
  one.
- **Used once, then forgotten, confirmed:** *« Je m'en servirais peut-être une
  fois par curiosité pour voir le nom qu'il donne à mon style. »* — "I'd use it
  maybe once, out of curiosity, to see what name it gives my style." The risk
  map put that at high likelihood. A real person confirmed it unprompted.
- **Cold start, confirmed:** *« J'utilise pas d'appli dédiée au style, j'ai la
  flemme. »* — "I don't use any style app, I can't be bothered." The reason no
  wardrobe app gets installed is not a missing feature. It is effort.
- **Fit, not style, is his test:** *« Je l'enfile et je regarde si je me sens à
  l'aise. Si ça me serre les épaules, je lâche l'affaire. »* — "I put it on and
  check whether I feel comfortable. If it's tight on the shoulders, I give up."
  The product reasons about ten style axes and knows nothing about comfort.

## The question I should have asked

> **« Tu aurais dû me demander la place que j'ai dans ma valise pour rentrer en
> France ! En échange, mon style il est surtout limité par les 23 kilos de la
> compagnie aérienne. »**
>
> *"You should have asked how much room I have in my suitcase to fly home. On
> exchange, my style is mostly limited by the airline's 23 kilos."*

Not a joke I would have modelled. For an exchange student the wardrobe is
capped by a suitcase, which is the same constraint as "style what you already
own", arriving from a direction no desk research would have found.

---

## Verdict and what changes

| | |
|---|---|
| **Assumption** | **Contradicted as written.** He names his style fluently; naming is not the problem. |
| **Falsifier** | **Triggered.** "Cheaper and faster, 100%." |
| **Kept** | The underlying cost is real: a garment bought, worn ten minutes, never again. |
| **Verdict on the record** | moved from **real** to **partly** — and saved as a new row in `research_records`, so the change is dated rather than remembered. |

What it changes in the product, in order:

1. **Price becomes the first constraint, not the fourth.** The budget chips
   start at €150; they should start far lower, and the cheapest option should
   be the default rather than the floor.
2. **Second-hand stops being a "later".** His default shop is Vinted; Mexico's
   equivalent, GoTrendier, is already row 9 of the competitor table with nine
   million users. The research and the interview arrived at the same place from
   opposite directions, which is the only reason I trust it.
3. **Fit and comfort are missing.** Ten style axes, and nothing about whether a
   shoulder seam is going to sit right.
4. **The pitch changes.** Not "we name your style". Closer to "we find what you
   already like, cheaper".

## What I got wrong in running it

The script asked for age and city and I recorded neither. Question 7 also gave
him the product and the question in the same breath, so his first reaction
("c'est marrant, le rendu est propre" — *"it's fun, the output looks clean"*)
is about the interface, not about whether he would use it. Next time: show it,
say nothing, and wait.

One conversation is one conversation. It cannot prove that no one needs the
Style Core; it can prove that this person does not, which is one more data
point than the project had yesterday.
