# Human Decision Note — Week 2

---

The rule I set before any code was written: **no claim without a source**. It cost rows. A repeated "7 million users" for Acloset traces back only
to rival apps' own pages, so Acloset sits there with no count.
Zalando's much-quoted "50% good outfits" is nowhere on Zalando's site; it
appears attributed to the 2018 S&P Global piece it comes from.

The correction I care about most was my own: the row "asking a friend" cited
this week's validation conversation before it had happened. It now carries his actual words.

I rejected scoring each competitor 0 to 100: it would look rigorous and mean
nothing, since I own no instrument that produces those numbers. The risk map is
a 3 × 3 grid for the same reason.

Then the conversation contradicted me. I had assumed people cannot name their
own direction. He named his in one sentence — *"relax et ample"* — and said
what he wants is cheaper and faster, not better taste: *"mon vrai problème,
c'est pas l'inspiration, c'est mon budget."* That is the falsifier I wrote into
the packet, and it triggered.

I am not deleting the assumption: the same person described buying a shirt he
wore ten minutes and never again. The problem is real in what he did and wrong
in how I framed it: not vocabulary, but something to stop him before paying. So the verdict on the saved record moved
from *real* to *partly*, and next week's question is price and second-hand, not
naming.
