# Human Decision Note — Week 3

---

The decision that set the rest: **the person this product is for is not the one
who pays for it.** Last week's conversation ended with *"acheter moins cher et
plus vite, 100 %"* — cheaper and faster, not better taste — and a walk-away
price of €150. Pricing a subscription at that person would have meant inventing
a customer two weeks after going to the trouble of finding a real one. So Free stays
free, the consumer tier is per lookbook, and the segment expected to pay is the
brand that already counts returns in pesos: fashion is 28% of Mexican online
returns, and 19.3% of online orders come back.

I rejected the figure every article repeats — that an apparel return costs a
retailer twenty to thirty dollars. Every trail led to content marketing, not a
study. It is in the model as a **guess** the reader can move, and the
difference is printed on the page.

The same rule produced the one feature I would defend hardest: the line that
bills a brand for returns avoided is set to **zero by default**. No brand has
agreed to it. A pricing page that collects revenue from a claim it has not
proven is a slide, not a model.

Three corrections came from reading screenshots rather than from assertions:
figures breaking mid-number, the revenue columns pushed off the card by a
table rule borrowed from another page, and amounts printed as a bare `$` on a
page that also quotes dollars and euros.
